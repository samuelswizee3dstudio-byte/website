The short version Almost everything happens in one place, Stripe. Products, prices, orders, refunds and the money all live there, and the website rebuilds itself from Stripe about five minutes after you change something. You never need to touch the website itself. The only things Stripe cannot do are the words on the About page and the legal pages, and adding a YouTube video. For those, ask Paul, or follow section 10.

2How the shop works

It helps to know the shape of it, because it explains why changes take a few minutes and why the sandbox matters so much.

  • Changes are not instant. The website waits until you have stopped editing for three minutes, then rebuilds once. Add ten products in a row and it updates once at the end. Allow about five minutes.
  • No card details ever touch the website. Payment happens on Stripe's page, so there is nothing for you to secure there.
  • The customer gets an email receipt from Stripe automatically. You do not need to send one.
  • How it reaches them is chosen by the customer at checkout. Either free local delivery, which is WA5 postcodes only and you take round yourself, or UK post by Royal Mail. Their address comes through with the order either way.

3Stripe: the sandbox trap

Stripe has a practice mode called a sandbox. It looks almost identical to the real thing, but nothing in it is real and nothing you add there will ever appear on the website. This has caught Paul more than once.

Before you do anything in Stripe, look at the top-left corner.

If you find yourself in the sandbox, the top-left will say Swizee 3D studio sandbox and there will be an orange bar across the top of every page saying you are testing in a sandbox. Click the name in the top-left and choose the plain Swizee 3D studio, or click Exit sandbox on the orange bar.

Anything you created while in there stays in the sandbox. You will need to add it again in the real account.

If something you added is not on the website Check this first, before anything else. Nine times out of ten, the product went into the sandbox.

4Adding something to sell

  1. Click Product catalog in the left-hand menu. (Stripe spells it the American way.)
  2. Click the purple Create product button, top right.
  1. A panel called Add a product slides in. Fill in:
    NameWhat it is called on the website. Keep it short: "Flexi Lizard", not "Articulated 3D Printed Flexi Lizard Toy".
    DescriptionA sentence or two. It shows under the name on the product page.
    ImageClick Upload. Square photos look best. You can add several, and people can click through them on the website.
  2. Under Pricing, click One-off. Stripe pre-selects "Recurring", which means a subscription, and the website ignores subscription prices. This is the step everyone misses.
  3. Type the Amount, like 4.50. Check it says GBP.
  4. Click Add product. Wait about five minutes, then look at the website.

That is all a simple item needs. Everything in the next section is only for special cases.

5Options, names and colours

Special behaviour is switched on with Metadata: little "left box, right box" pairs that Stripe stores against a product. The website reads them. On a new product they are under More options, just below the image; on an existing product there is a Metadata panel on the right-hand side with a pencil to edit it.

Things where the customer types a name

Like the name clicker keyrings. Add one line of metadata:

Left boxRight box
personalisetrueThe website adds a box for the customer to type in, allows letters and numbers only, and puts what they typed on your order.
personalise_labelName to printOptional. Changes the wording above that box from "Name or word to print".

Options that cost different amounts

Like a keyring where 3 letters costs £4.50 and 4 letters costs £5.00. Do not make two products. Make one product with several prices:

  1. Add the product as normal with the first price, then open it from the catalogue.
  2. Next to the Pricing heading, click the + button. A panel called Add price to … opens.
  3. Click One-off (again, Recurring is pre-selected). Type the amount.
  4. Scroll down to Advanced and put the option's wording in Price description: 3 letters, 4 letters and so on. Use wording that contains the number. The website reads the number out of it and stops customers typing a longer name than they have paid for.
  5. Click Create price. Repeat for each option.

Letting the customer choose a colour

Add one line to the product's metadata listing the colours separated by commas:

Left boxRight box
coloursBlack, White, Blue, PinkGives a drop-down. The customer must pick one before adding to basket, and their choice appears on your order.
colour_labelBase colourOptional. Wording above the drop-down, instead of "Colour".
colours_2Orange, White, GreenA second drop-down, for things with two colours, like the base and the letters of a name clicker.
colour_2_labelLetter colourWording above the second one.
family_discounttrueCounts towards the family discount: 10% off when somebody buys three or more of these between them. Both name clickers already have it.
  • Spell the colours exactly how you want to read them on the order.
  • Colours do not change the price. If one colour costs more, that is a price option instead (above).
  • To stop offering a colour, delete it from the list. Orders already placed keep the colour they were bought with. Delete the whole colours line and the drop-down disappears.

6Changing a price, removing an item

Changing a price

Stripe will not let you change the amount of a price once it exists. Instead you add the new one and retire the old one:

  1. Open the product. Click the + next to Pricing and add the new price (One-off, amount, and the same Price description if it is an option).
  2. Find the old price in the list, click its … menu on the right, and choose Archive price.
Both steps matter If you forget to archive the old one, the website shows both prices as options and customers can pick the cheaper.

Taking something off the website

Just for now (sold out, out of filament): open the product, edit its Metadata, and add hidden = true. It stays in Stripe with all its history but disappears from the website. Delete that line to bring it back.

For good: open the product, click the … button at the top right next to Edit product, and choose Archive product.

7Reading an order

Stripe emails you when someone pays. To see the orders, click Transactions in the left-hand menu; the Payments tab is the list of orders, newest first.

On the order page, the Checkout summary in the middle has everything you need:

  • The customer's name, email, phone number and address.
  • What they bought, how many, and what they paid.
  • How they want it, on the Shipping line: either Free local delivery (WA5 postcodes) or UK delivery — Royal Mail 2nd Class. Either way the address is under Shipping details. Check the postcode on a local one. Stripe will not let the website limit that option by postcode, so somebody further away can choose it by mistake. If they have, message them: either they pay the £3.50 postage or you refund the order. The terms of sale say so.

If they ordered anything personalised or chose colours, look at the Metadata box in the right-hand column, below Customer. It looks like this:

item_1Name Clicker Keyring (3 letters) ×2: BEN — Base colour: Black, Letter colour: Orange
item_2Flexi Lizard: Colour: Purple
items_to_personalise2

That is exactly what to print. ×2 means they ordered two of that one.

When the order is ready: local delivery, take it round; post, send it second class. Stripe has already sent them a receipt.

8Refunds and getting paid

Giving a refund

Open the order under Transactions and click Refund at the top right. You can refund all of it or part of it. The money goes back to the customer's card in a few days and Stripe emails them.

You do not have to print something rude or impossible. Refund it and say why. The terms of sale on the website already allow this.

Stripe keeps its fee on a refund Stripe charges a small fee on every payment (a few pence plus a percentage). When you refund, the customer gets the full amount back but Stripe does not return its fee, so a refunded order costs you a few pence. Paul's test order in September 2026 left the balance at minus 24p for this reason; it clears against the next sale.

Where the money goes

Stripe collects the payments and pays them out to your bank account on a rolling schedule. Balances in the left-hand menu shows what is waiting; Transactions → Payouts lists each transfer to the bank. The bank account itself is under Settings (the cog, top right) → Linked accounts and payouts, or directly at dashboard.stripe.com/settings/payouts. The Settlement and transfer rules tab on the same page holds the payout schedule.

9If something is not showing up

Work down this list, in order:

  1. Are you in the sandbox? Check the top-left says Swizee 3D studio with no coloured bar. This is the most common cause by a long way.
  2. Wait five more minutes and refresh the page.
  3. Is the product Active? Archived products do not show. In the catalogue, click Archived to check.
  4. Does it have a price, in GBP, as a One-off? No price, a price in dollars, or a Recurring price, and it will not appear.
  5. Did you add hidden = true? Check the Metadata panel.
  6. Still stuck? Ask Paul, and say what you did and when.
Web addresses Each product's address, like swizee.co.uk/products/flexi-lizard, comes from its name. Renaming "Axolotl" to "Axolotl Buddy" changes the address, and any link you already posted stops working. If you are going to share a link on Instagram, first add metadata slug = axolotl to pin the address, and never change it.

10Changing the words on the website

The front page, the About page, the contact page, the terms and the privacy policy are not in Stripe. The normal route is to ask Paul: send him the new wording and he will put it up.

If Paul is not around, you can do it yourself. The wording lives in five plain text files on GitHub, one per page, and editing one rebuilds the website in a few minutes. It looks technical but it is a text box and a Save button.

Front pagehome.md
About pageabout.md (also lists the YouTube videos)
Contact pagecontact.md
Terms of saleterms.md
Privacy policyprivacy.md
  1. Sign in to github.com with the Gmail account.
  2. Go to github.com/samuelswizee3dstudio-byte/website/tree/main/src/content/copy and click the file you want.
  3. Click the pencil icon (top right of the file, "Edit this file").
  4. Change the words. Leave the lines between the --- marks at the top alone unless you are adding a video (below). Blank lines make paragraphs; a line starting with ## is a heading.
  5. Click the green Commit changes… button, then Commit changes again in the box that appears. Leave "Commit directly to the main branch" selected.
  6. Wait about three minutes and refresh the website.

Adding a YouTube video to the About page

Upload the video to YouTube first. Its address will look like youtube.com/watch?v=k3Fd5xHrOBE; the bold part is the video's ID. Then edit about.md and, in the block between the --- marks at the top, add two lines under videos: in exactly this shape (two spaces before the dash, four before "title"):

videos: - id: k3Fd5xHrOBE title: "Printing a name clicker base" - id: NEWVIDEOID title: "What the new one is called"

Commit as above. To remove a video, delete its two lines. If you get the spacing wrong the website build fails and nothing changes; it does not break the live site. Ask Paul if that happens.

Do not edit anything outside that folder Every other file in GitHub is code. A change there can take the site down, and the fix is Paul's, not yours.
